## Mindfulness
Mindfulness is about being fully present in the moment, without judging or judging, and regardless of whether it is pleasant or unpleasant. Mindfulness is about attention. For example, if you go for a walk, but your brain is filled with thoughts of, for example, an upcoming competition or yesterday's conflict with the coach, you probably don't find the walk particularly rewarding.
Being able to direct attention to what is happening right now is considered very important for well-being, but also for being able to perform well. However, it is important in this context to realize that thoughts tend to drift off to situations that (maybe) will happen, or that have happened, and that this in itself is not something abnormal or negative, but even human. With mindfulness training, you can learn to take note of when your thoughts fly away and get back to the present moment faster.
Mindfulness is also about accepting both external situations (for example, that the weather is bad or that an injury has occurred) and of inner experiences (such as thoughts and feelings). Acceptance of inner experiences means that you can experience feelings and thoughts that come up in a certain situation, such as nervousness before a match, without having to change that feeling or judge it as negative. Being able to just look at what is here and now without judging, leads to you having a better opportunity to act in the way you really want, and that you are not controlled by your thoughts and feelings.
What can you do yourself?
- Practice mindfulness and preferably do it regularly and over a longer period of time. A suitable amount of training can be 4-5 training sessions per week, preferably 10-15 minutes.
- If you feel that this is difficult to achieve, start with slightly shorter exercises, or fewer training sessions, and strive to gradually increase to the above mentioned amount.
- There are different types of mindfulness exercises: meditations, everyday exercises, and short exercises to stop. Try different ones and see which exercises suit you!
- Practice stopping in everyday life, just for a short moment, and become aware of that particular moment: "What do I feel, what do I think, what do I hear, what do I see?" Make a note for yourself, and then return to what you were doing before.
- Download an app for your mindfulness training. There are some apps that contain mindfulness exercises (some examples are Mindfully or Mindfulness in Swedish, or Smiling Mind, Calm, Mindfulness.com or Headspace in English). Pick one and start training. You can also get help with reminders so that the training actually happens.
