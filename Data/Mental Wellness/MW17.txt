## Screen Time and Social Media

Screen time refers to the time spent in front of different types of screens, such as a mobile phone, tablet or computer. The technological development in our society has led to previously unimagined opportunities and we are now more or less tied to all those possibilities. There is a sometimes emotionally charged discussion in society when it comes to the use of screens and whether it is good or bad to stare at them. It is difficult to answer the question, partly because screen time can involve many different activities and that research has not yet provided completely clear answers.

However, one factor that can be good to consider is that mobiles and other portable gadgets increase the ability to always be contactable and that this constant availability can lead to high levels of stress. Compared to previous periods, where we could neither read emails nor perform tasks everywhere and around the clock, there are now no such natural time-outs. This can be an important factor in connection with recovery, which is an important piece of the puzzle for elite athletes.

The use of social media, such as Facebook and Instagram, is another area that has been discussed. One of the most central reasons for social media's success is the brain's reward system and social acceptance. A "like" represents an approval of another person, an incredibly powerful social reward, which triggers the secretion of hormones in the reward system. This can become addictive, which explains why it is so difficult to stop using this type of media.

An important aspect of social media is the ability to compare yourself to others and to see how (mostly "good") everyone else is doing. The constant comparison and information about how others, such as competitors, are doing, can lead to high stress in the individual. But of course, it is not possible to draw general conclusions here either, but the effect depends on the individual's use of social media and how the individual interprets what they are taking part in.

What we recommend is simply to start reflecting on your screen time and social media usage habits, especially if you're feeling stressed or anxious and want to optimize your recovery or sleep. Think about how you can limit your time on social media and especially in connection with sleeping.

## What can you do yourself to reduce Screen Time and Social Media?

- Map your social media usage. Many mobiles and computers now have apps that map how often you visit different apps and sit in front of different screens. Then you get black and white how much time you spend in front of screens and on social media.
- Create free time. If you feel that you spend too much time in front of screens or social media, try putting your mobile away, or turning off your social media accounts, during certain set hours of the day. One tip is to put your phone away at least an hour before bedtime, as there is reason to believe that screens can activate the brain, which counteracts sleep.
- Buy an alarm clock and a wristwatch so you don't have to look at your phone for functions you don't need it for.
- Place your phone outside your bedroom. This can help if you have trouble sleeping or if you simply want to unwind a little more. If you need to have your phone in the bedroom, turn off the sound or set your phone to do not disturb.
- Do not open (work) emails before going to bed.
- Turn off push notifications that grab your attention every time something new happens.
- Keep your phone on silent when you want to be concentrated on something else (e.g. during exercise). Otherwise, there is a risk that you will start to wonder what just beeped and then your attention will be split.
- Set aside time to check text messages and emails, but then put your mobile away and turn off the email program when you want to focus on work or school.
- Think through how you want to use social media before competitions/championships. It can be stressful to read about your competitors and their ("perfect") workouts.
- Try making more conscious choices about how you use the technology over the course of a week and see what you discover.
