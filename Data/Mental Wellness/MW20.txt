## Stress and exhaustion

There are two areas that you can work on: one relates to how you can reduce your stress and strain, the other is about building good routines for recovery. 

## How to Reduce stress and strain:

Lower your demands. A common problem, not least among elite athletes who tend to strive for high goals, is to set very high standards for themselves, in many or all areas of life. Try to find out if there are areas where you can lower your requirements. If you can settle for a "good enough" effort in an area of life that is not a high priority at the moment, you can have time for other things, such as recovery. A good thing to do is to test your own thoughts before doing a task "good enough": What do you think will happen when you do something with the attitude that good enough is enough? Try doing a task with that setting. Then evaluate whether what you expected happened and whether the attitude had a positive impact on your own health. Practice saying no. If you are a person who often takes on many tasks and stands up for others, you may need to practice saying no. It may feel uncomfortable at first, and you may think that a no will lead to trouble. But if you don't test, you don't know. Therefore, practice saying no. Then evaluate how it went: did it lead to trouble? And keep in mind that it is possible to say no in a friendly way. Prioritize What is most important and what can be done later or refrained from? Perfectionism leads to high stress. Practice lowering the expectations you have of yourself.

Reduce screen time. Being reachable and connected all the time can be a source of stress. If you read work emails at all times of the day and spend time on social media following (and stressed) what your competitors are up to, it might be wise to set aside screen-free periods during the day. A tip that can positively affect your sleep is to have screen-free time an hour or so before bedtime. Also, make sure to have your bedroom free of screens so as not to be distracted by them during the night. Turn off push notifications on your mobile to avoid a constant flow from your social media. Feel free to refrain from social media before major championships and competitions, i.e. suspend your account temporarily to avoid the stress that the constant comparison creates. 

## How to bet on your recovery:

Improve your sleep habits. Sleep is our main recovery strategy and is needed for the body and brain to rest, recover and process impressions. The need for sleep is different, but high physical exertion, which is a given part of a sports life, increases the need for sleep.

Find your own recovery strategies. Think about which activities are restorative and relaxing for you. Maybe it's a walk in nature, reading a book, listening to music, gardening or spending time with family and friends? List your top recovery strategies and try to schedule them into your everyday life, just like you book training and school or work activities. It is important that recovery activities are free from demands on performance.

Practice de-stressing. When we're stressed, we tend to do things extra quickly: we talk faster, walk faster, eat faster, and try to optimize time. Try to stop this pattern and slow down your pace. Walk a little slower towards the bus, take your time to eat and chew properly, stop for conversation and try to slow down your speaking pace.

Learn some kind of relaxation exercise, such as deep breathing or progressive muscle relaxation. Get a routine into your everyday life to do such a relaxation exercise. It will "take" your time, but in exchange you will have more energy and can perform upcoming tasks in a better way, saving you time in the long run.

Practice mindfulness regularly. Mindfulness is about the ability to be here and now, in the moment. When stressed, it is often the case that thoughts take over and you worry about things that have already happened or things that might happen. Research shows that those who regularly train the ability to be here and now, can reduce their stress level. Doing a mindfulness 
practice can also give you a short, much-needed break during the day.

Think about your relaxation strategies. It can be tempting to try to relax during periods of stress with the help of various substances, such as alcohol and tobacco. These can be relaxing in the moment, but increase the levels of stress hormones in the blood and can over time increase your sensitivity to stress. If you notice that you need to ingest any substance to relax, we advise you to find other options for your recovery.

## List of exercises that can help you deal with stress and exhaustion.

The progressive muscle relaxation exercise is a method that aims to relax muscles. Emotional stress and anxiety are most often linked to increased muscle tension and physiological activation. By reducing the muscular tension, emotional tension and anxiety can also be reduced. The exercise involves first tensing a certain muscle group for about 10 seconds, and then relaxing the muscle group.
