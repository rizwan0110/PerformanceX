## Anxiety and worry

Fear is a natural part of being human: a way for the body to tell you that something is dangerous and that you need to act. Fear can be vital in certain situations, it acts as a fire alarm and helps you seek shelter. But when the body sounds the alarm too often, and in really harmless situations, you may have developed an anxiety problem.

Anxiety is the most common mental illness in Sweden; Around 25 percent suffer from an anxiety disorder at some point in their lives. The incidence of the disease has been shown to be about as high among elite athletes.

## What can you do yourself to deal with anxiety and worry?

Explore your behavior. Anxiety can lead you to avoid things, which in turn can lead to your life being less rewarding and less meaningful. Over time, your performance can deteriorate because of this, and in the long run, it can lead to depression. It is therefore important that you discover what you have started to avoid and that you resume these activities and behaviors again. Although it feels uncomfortable at first, it will increase your faith in yourself and help reduce your anxiety over time.
Explore your anxiety. When you feel strong anxiety, try to stop, be curious about how you experience the anxiety and describe to yourself what is happening. What are your thoughts and feelings? How does the anxiety feel in the body? Allow the anxiety to be part of what is right now. Practice mindfulness to help you focus on the present moment. Anxiety is often about things that (maybe) happen in the future, so if you manage to get back to the present, the anxiety can decrease. Make sure to rest and get enough sleep. Train yourself in relaxation. Work actively with recovery. If you are rested and relaxed, there is less risk of getting anxiety.

## When and where should you seek help in case of Anxiety and worry?

If you feel very unwell and need to get in direct contact with the healthcare system, you should seek emergency care.

If the anxiety feels difficult to deal with, if you notice that you are starting to avoid one or more things or situations in your (sports) life, and if you have difficulty functioning normally in your everyday life, we recommend that you seek help from the healthcare system.

If you feel anxious, for example in the form of nervousness before competitions, and start to consider avoiding certain situations in your life (e.g. competitions or camps), we recommend that you seek support in the form of sports psychology counseling.
