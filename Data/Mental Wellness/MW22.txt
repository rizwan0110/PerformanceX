## Sleep: Sleep is a basic human need and a basis for your well-being. If sleep is not working satisfactorily, it can significantly limit you in your everyday life. Additionally, insufficient sleep over time can lead to various mental illnesses, such as anxiety and depression. For athletes, sleeping well can be a challenge, especially during phases of tough training, travel, and competitions. Athletes who train hard and a lot also tend to have a greater need for sleep than people who don't train as hard. Adequate sleep is important to avoid overtraining and to maximize the effect of the exercise. Insufficient sleep is linked to poorer performance. 

## What can you do yourself to improve ?

Reduce the stress in your life. Sleep problems are often associated with stress and anxiety. If this is the case for you, you need to think through what the stress and worry are basically about and try to solve the problem. Do quiet activities before bedtime. To help the body go into a resting state, it can be good to unwind during the evening. Examples of calming activities before bedtime include reading, drinking (decaffeinated) tea and taking a warm bath. Doing a relaxation exercise or a mindfulness exercise is also good. If you write down things that you need to remember for tomorrow on a piece of paper, it will be easier not to think about the next day, and to relax. Dim the lights before bedtime. Bright light makes you more alert, which counteracts sleep. This also applies to the light from a screen.
Avoid all forms of caffeine (e.g. coffee, black tea, soda or energy drink) in the hours before bed. Also try to reduce the amount of caffeine during the day, as caffeine remains in the body for a long time. Put your hard workouts early in the day and, if possible, avoid all exercise in the hours before you go to sleep.Make the bed a quiet and relaxing place. Make sure it is dark, quiet and cool where you are going to sleep. Don't read work/school documents in bed. Keep your phone out of the bedroom and, if possible, remove clocks so you don't stress yourself over time. Don't stay in bed if you can't fall asleep. If you lie awake in bed for half an hour, we recommend you to get up instead, and do a calming activity. Then go to bed as soon as you feel sleepy. The bed should be a place that you associate with sleeping, not a place where you lie awake and brood. Ideally, avoid taking a nap if you suffer from sleep problems, as it can impair your chances of falling asleep well at night. If you still want to take a nap for your recovery, you should keep the following in mind: take a short nap (maximum 30 min.) early in the day, for example around lunchtime.

## When and where should you seek help if you have difficulty regrading sleep?

When you have trouble falling asleep and waking up during the night or too early in the morning, and if this pattern has been going on regularly for more than four weeks and is significantly affecting your everyday functioning (exercise, school, work, etc.), we recommend that you seek help from a healthcare professional.
If you feel that your sleep does not give you the rest you need, but you are not regularly bothered by the above-mentioned symptoms and do not believe that the problems affect how you function in everyday life, we recommend that you seek support in the form of sports psychology counseling.
