## Self-compassion contains three different parts: mindfulness, common humanity and self-kindness.

## Mindfulness is about being fully present in a situation, even if it involves suffering (e.g. after a failure), and being so without judgment. Mindfulness is central to self-compassion, because until you've become aware of the situation, and your inner reactions to it, you can't help yourself. Letting unpleasant thoughts and feelings be, and having the insight that they are transient experiences that can be allowed to exist, is an important first step.

## Common humanity. Realizing that failures, inadequacies and shortcomings are part of being human, and that everyone experiences it from time to time, is also an important insight. Maybe you can feel lonely in difficult periods, think that you are alone in experiencing what you are facing right now. Then it helps to understand that we are all in the same boat and that it may even be the case that there is someone in your immediate surroundings who has experienced or is experiencing the same thing as you.

## Self-kindness is about treating yourself with warmth, care, and acceptance, as we would treat our best friend who is struggling. Instead of having that critical inner dialogue after a failure, why not try to talk to yourself just like you would talk to a close friend who has experienced the same thing?

## Compassion is about meeting other people with kindness, care and understanding, which can be an important approach to creating safe and positive environments, for example in clubs and national team groups.
