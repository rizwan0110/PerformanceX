## Sleep: Sleep is a basic human need and an important basis for your well-being. Sleep is needed for your body and your brain to recover and process impressions. When you get enough sleep, you'll be able to handle stress and strain during the day more easily. For you as an athlete, enough sleep is important to avoid overtraining-related problems and to maximize the effect of the training. Too little sleep for a long time can make it difficult to function well in everyday life and is linked to the onset of anxiety, depression, and exhaustion, for example.

It is therefore wise to take care of your sleep and get good sleep routines. The need for sleep is individual, but high physical strain can increase the need for sleep. A simple rule is that if you feel alert, work well during the day and have the energy for your training, you are likely to get enough sleep. The body is very smart and strives to "catch up" with the sleep needed; If you sleep poorly one night, this is compensated by sleeping deeper the next night – the problem is usually "fixed" without you having to think about it.

It's important not to let your sleep become yet another area where you have to "perform". Instead, try to have a relaxed approach to your sleep. If you feel alert, you don't need to spend any time thinking about your sleep, just carry on as before. If, on the other hand, you feel that you are tired, then try to improve your sleep routines with one or more of the tips below. Forcing the perfect sleep usually worsens sleep and puts even more pressure on you. You may have noticed that it becomes more difficult to fall asleep when you lie sleepless in bed at night and think: "Now I HAVE to sleep". Don't complicate it unnecessarily and avoid focusing on different aids and measuring instruments that show how you sleep, as these can create additional stress.

## Tips for Improving Sleep for Athletes and High Performers

- Schedule enough time for sleep. Try to avoid (repetitive) activities late at night so that you have the opportunity to unwind and fall asleep. If you have late training and/or match times, you may especially need to add cooling activities.
- Have regular sleep times. Try as much as possible to have regular times for when you go to bed and when you wake up.
- Do quiet activities before bedtime. To help your body go into hibernation during the evening, it can be helpful to unwind with calming activities, such as reading, drinking (decaffeinated) tea, and taking a hot bath. A relaxation exercise or a mindfulness exercise can be helpful before bedtime.
- Dim the lights before bedtime. Bright light makes you perk up. Even the light from a screen can counteract sleep.
- Make sure you have cooled in the bedroom.
- Make the bed a quiet and relaxing place. Make sure to decorate the bedroom in a place where you feel safe and comfortable. If you are at longer training camps/competitions, try as much as possible to decorate the bedroom pleasantly. You may want to bring something from home to make the room safer.
- Think about whether the use of mobile phones, tablets and computers affects your sleep. How long before going to sleep is it advisable to have screen-free time?
- Prepare for stays in other time zones. If you are going to travel to competitions or camps that involve a new circadian rhythm, you can prepare for that transition by thinking about how best to sleep in the days before and during the trip, and how you can best get into the new circadian rhythm.
