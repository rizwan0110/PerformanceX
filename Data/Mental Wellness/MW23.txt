## Addictions: An addiction means that it is difficult or impossible to reduce or stop a behavior. You can become addicted to various substances (e.g. alcohol, nicotine, cannabis, heroin) or to activities (e.g. gambling). The craving for the substance or activity can become so strong that it is difficult to abstain from it, even though it can have negative consequences. For example, work, school, finances or social interaction can be affected. It can also lead to not doing other important activities or caring about important relationships. Life is increasingly becoming about the substance/activity, while other areas of life are becoming less interesting. Ingestion of the substance and completion of the activity lead to feeling better, feeling relaxed or having fewer negative feelings and thoughts in the short term. Because it feels positive, it makes you continue with the behavior. There is not much research on addiction problems among elite athletes, but it is believed to be relatively common in some sports.

## Alcohol addictions symptoms

- You feel a strong craving or craving for alcohol.
- You drink more alcohol or for longer than you intended.
- You do not succeed in reducing your drinking even though alcohol gives you physical or mental problems.
- Drinking takes on a central role in your life, often at the expense of other things that you think are important.
- You have to drink more alcohol than before to get drunk.
- You feel bad when you don't drink alcohol and experience withdrawal symptoms (e.g. tremors, nausea or difficulty sleeping).

## Gambling addictions symptoms

- You're constantly thinking about games and how you're going to get money for the next game.
- To keep the excitement, it requires increasing amounts of money to play with.
- You don't manage to reduce gambling even if you want to.
- You become restless or irritable when you try to limit or stop gambling.
- Gambling has become a way to avoid thinking about problems or to get away from anxiety or depression.
- You continue to play even though you lose money, in order to win back lost money.
- You lie to others to hide how much you gamble.
- You jeopardize or lose any important personal relationship, employment, opportunity for education or career because of gambling.
- You rely on others to provide money to solve a financial crisis that has arisen due to gambling.

## What can you do in case of addiction?

- Start with a completely sober period or cut down on your consumption significantly if it feels too difficult to quit completely. After such a period, it may be easier to continue to change habits. Set a goal that is realistic and achievable, for you. It is a good idea to start with a short period and then extend the time period.
- Keep an alcohol diary. Take note of what and when you drink, so you get a better overview and control and can clearly see when you are achieving your goals.
- Think about what situations and social interactions involve a risk of you drinking too much. Try to minimize the number of times you end up in these situations.
Be prepared. Think in advance about what alternatives you have to drink alcohol at times when you know it may be difficult to abstain. For example, can you choose a non-alcoholic beverage, engage in other activities or hang out with people you don't usually drink alcohol with?
- Talk to loved ones and ask them for help. For example, say that you want to drink less, or not at all, and tell them how you want them to support you in this.
- Keep a game diary. Take note of whether you have played, what made you play, how much you played for and how long you played. Also note how you felt before, during and after playing. The diary can be helpful in understanding what triggers the urge to gamble and what you can do to avoid ending up in these situations. It can also be good to write when you haven't played and how it felt. Write down how you managed not to play, so you can use it as a tip and do the same thing again.
- Set goals. It can be about abstaining from gambling for a whole day. Feel free to start with small and simple goals, then you can gradually set higher goals.
- Start thinking about what activities you think are fun outside of gaming. Getting involved in these activities will help you think about something other than gambling.
- Talking to someone about your gambling can help. Talk to a loved one or contact a support association. Support associations are located in different places in the country.
- Turn off certain sites on your browser to avoid being attracted to gambling. You can also disconnect completely from the internet to prevent gambling.
It is not uncommon to exchange one addiction for another. Therefore, be observant so that you do not seek out alcohol, drugs or other things that can be addictive.
