## How should we deal with our nervousness?
- Learn more about mindfulness, an approach that is good for dealing with different emotions.
- Practice developing an accepting approach to your feelings, thoughts and bodily sensations before a competition. Do not try to change, remove or minimize your nervousness, but instead accept it and learn to understand that nervousness is a completely natural and harmless process.
- Practice observing what is happening, without judging. To be able to accept your feelings, thoughts and bodily sensations, you must learn to recognize them when they arise in a competitive situation (or any other situation where you get nervous, for example when you have to give a presentation at work/school or a number). For example, you can speak quietly to yourself, say what you experience, in the style of "now I feel my heart pounding". Once you observe what is happening, try to be permissive, for example by telling yourself that "it's perfectly okay to feel this way".
- Once you have accepted that you feel the way you do, you can bring your attention to what is most important to you in the moment: for example, performing a good warm-up, preparing your material, listening to the coach's instructions, or performing the behaviors in your sport that you have decided on.
- To make it easier to know what to focus your attention on, it's good to have a clear plan: Be careful in deciding what your preparation for the competition will look like. Be clear about how you want to act during the competition. It can be about specific process goals that refer to, for example, your shot plan (if you are a shooter), your race plan (if you are a runner, cyclist, swimmer, skier), your match plan (if you are a team athlete, tennis player) or simply specific behaviors that you want to achieve at the competition (for example, a special moment in the approach to your swimming jump or how you should act when the opponent attacks you in a certain way in martial arts). Describe the specific behaviors you will perform and note them on a piece of paper to bring to the competition. The note can be good to take out if you get nervous and have difficulty thinking clearly.
- Learn about and practice self-compassion, an approach that involves treating yourself in a kind way especially in connection with setbacks, difficulties and other challenging periods. Self-compassion can help you deal with your nervousness.
