## Exercises to increase acceptance and create distance to inner experiences.

The following exercises aim to open up to our inner experiences (for example thoughts, feelings, memories, past experiences, desires and sensations) and let them be as they are, without fighting against or trying to change them. The exercises also help you practice keeping a distance from your inner experiences. Both acceptance of and distance from your inner experiences are important skills for increasing your psychological flexibility. A situation where acceptance can be particularly helpful is in case of nervousness, a feeling that most people experience in connection with a competition, match or championship.
- Acceptance exercise: In the exercise, you practice being accepting of the experiences you have right now. Acceptance is an important skill for increasing psychological flexibility. Set aside some time (approx. 7 min.), make sure you can be undisturbed and put your mobile phone on silent. Start the audio file by clicking the link https://utbildning.sisuforlag.se/globalassets/rfsisu/publik/idrottspsykologi/ljudfiler/acceptansovning.mp3
- Emotion detection: In the exercise, you practice noting, naming and accepting a particular feeling that you have at the moment. The exercise is particularly suitable in a situation where you experience a distinct feeling, for example, nervousness, which many feel before a competition or match. The purpose of the exercise is not to get rid of the feeling, but rather to be able to accept it, allow it, which makes it easier for you to be able to carry out what you want.
- Set aside some time (about 3 min. ), make sure you can be undisturbed, and put your mobile phone on silent. Start the audio file by clicking the link. https://utbildning.sisuforlag.se/globalassets/rfsisu/publik/idrottspsykologi/ljudfiler/kanslospaning-kort-variant.mp3
- Notice the competition: The purpose of the exercise is to practice noting the thoughts that appear, as well as to learn to distance yourself from your thoughts. Set aside some time (approx. 6 min.), make sure you can be undisturbed, and put your mobile phone on silent. - Notice thoughts: In the exercise, you practice observing and noticing your thoughts and how they come and go.
