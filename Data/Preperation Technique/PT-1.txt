## Prepare for Championship

If you are well prepared before an important competition or match, your chances of performing at your very best increase. In addition to the basic requirement, that you have had a good training plan before the championship, which included all physical, technical, tactical and psychological parts, the championship itself requires specific preparation. The vast majority of athletes feel good about being well prepared, for example knowing when the competition starts and how the transport to the arena works. Good preparation can create a feeling of security over a situation that is actually very uncertain and difficult to predict. However, the preparations should not be too square and rigid, because if deviations occur, it risks being perceived as disasters, which can be counterproductive to the performance. A good approach is to prepare as best you can, but in all parts try to find some flexible solutions that can be used if plan A does not work.

## What can you do yourself?

- Plan your race day in advance. Anything you can prepare will facilitate your race day and create space for you to focus on your performance. Perhaps you can also find it reassuring to have a clear plan for when and where things will happen.
- Find out the start time for your competition and any bus times or other transport to the arena. If you need (a lot of) material in your sport, decide how you will do with the transport and how you will arrange the equipment and store it.
- Decide when to get up in the morning.
- Plan all your meals.
- Think about what your warm-up should look like and sketch roughly how long it will take. This makes it easier when you have to plan the day of the competition. Warming up in the same way, regardless of whether it is a big championship or a small competition, is recommended, as routines create security.
- For any plans you create, also consider flexible options that can be used when things don't go as planned. For example, how should you warm up when the time for some reason is not enough? What is most important? What do you do if you oversleep?
- Be prepared for changed plans. If you are active in a sport where the weather (or any other factor) can change the start time, think about how you will handle a possible postponement of the competition. Where will you wait and what will you do in the meantime? By imagining a few scenarios, you will be able to handle the situation more easily when it does occur.
- Familiarize yourself with the arena. Maybe you have already raced there or you need to search for information about it, for example pictures so you can see what it looks like.
- Think about how you want to be at championships. Which values ​​are important to you and how do you want to be perceived in the competition situation? These questions can be interesting to get answers to before the competition, because the answers can help you behave in a way that feels right and important to you.
- Learn more about goal setting, and set goals for the competition.
- Learn more about nervousness, and prepare ahead of time how you want to deal with it.
- Learn more about concentration, and train your concentration in advance.
- Learn more about self-compassion and how you can treat yourself in a kind way.
